1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation

Campus hostel administration involves thousands of residents distributed across multiple blocks. Conventional systems relying on paper registers or flat spreadsheets face critical relational shortcomings:

  • Room Overbooking Anomalies: Rooms are mistakenly assigned beyond their physical architectural bed limits.
  • Referential Inconsistencies: Vacated beds leave orphaned complaint tickets or unchecked out-station gate passes.
  • Functional Redundancy: Room tiers, capacities, and amenities duplicated across resident rows violate 3NF.
  • Audit Failures: Daily night roll-call lacks idempotency, causing duplicated or omitted records.
  • Privilege Leakage: Without role-segregated tables, students can manipulate parameters to inspect other residents' private records.

HostelOS solves these systemic failures through a 10-table relational schema normalized to Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF / 3NF), enforcing foreign keys, cascading deletions, domain check constraints, automated triggers, and role-based data isolation.

4. Normalization Proofs (1NF through BCNF)

First Normal Form (1NF): All attributes contain atomic values. No multivalued repeating groups exist; past bed stays are isolated in allocations.

Second Normal Form (2NF): No partial functional dependencies. In composite candidate key tables (e.g. attendance), non-prime attributes depend on the complete key (student_id, date).

Third Normal Form (3NF) & BCNF: Eliminates transitive dependencies. Specifically, room_types is separated so type_name -> capacity is not transitive in rooms. Every determinant is a candidate key.
